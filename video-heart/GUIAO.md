## Hook  →  `scene_00_hook.mp3`

Your heart beats about a hundred thousand times a day. And in every single beat, there is a moment, shorter than the blink of an eye, when a hit to the chest can stop it. Twenty milliseconds. This is the physics of that moment.

## 2 · An Electric Pump  →  `scene_02_electric.mp3`

Your heart is a pump, but it is driven by electricity. A small cluster of cells fires a signal about once a second. A wave of charge sweeps across the heart, and the muscle contracts. Then every cell has to reset, pumping charged ions back into place. On an E C G, that reset is the T wave. And while the cells are resetting, not all of them are ready at the same time.

*Visual:* 3D heart, translucent; electrical wave (cyan) sweeping from the SA node across the chambers. ECG builds below: P, QRS, T labelled.

## 3 · The Window  →  `scene_03_window.mp3`

That uneven moment is the vulnerable window. In laboratory studies, it sits on the rising slope of the T wave, roughly ten to forty milliseconds wide. Only one to three percent of each heartbeat. A sudden blow over the heart stretches the muscle, and stretch-sensitive channels in the cells open. Inside the window, that can fire an extra beat at exactly the wrong time. The organized wave breaks into chaos. Doctors call it ventricular fibrillation. The heart quivers instead of pumping.

*Visual:* ECG zoom on the T wave; a narrow band lights up red: VULNERABLE WINDOW 10–40 ms. Cells as a grid resetting unevenly; impact → wave breaks into spirals (fibrillation).

## 4 · The Impact  →  `scene_04_impact.mp3`

It doesn't take a huge force. The blow has to land directly over the heart, and it has to be concentrated. A ball, a knee, a shoulder, an elbow. In animal studies, a baseball-sized impact was enough. Timing matters more than strength. That is why it is so rare, and so random. In January 2023, American football player Damar Hamlin collapsed after a tackle. Medics restarted his heart on the field. He survived, and went back to playing.

*Visual:* Chest cross-section: ribs, heart behind; a ball/knee hits — pressure wave. Comparison: same hit at a safe time (nothing) vs inside the window (VF). Timeline card: JAN 2023 · NFL · SURVIVED.

## 5 · The Clock  →  `scene_05_aed.mp3`

Once the heart fibrillates, a clock starts. No blood reaches the brain. For every minute without defibrillation, the chance of survival drops by roughly seven to ten percent. In one review of these cases, resuscitation within three minutes saved about a quarter of victims. After three minutes, only three percent. A defibrillator delivers one strong shock that resets every cell at once, giving the heart's own pacemaker a clean start.

*Visual:* Big countdown clock; survival % bar dropping 7–10% per minute. AED shock: whole heart flashes white, then a clean ECG rhythm returns.

## 6 · Two Minutes  →  `scene_06_outro.mp3`

You can't predict the twenty milliseconds. But you can change the minutes that follow. Know where the nearest defibrillator is. Learn C P R. Modern defibrillators talk you through every step. A heartbeat is electricity. And sometimes, the right shock, at the right time, is enough to bring it back.

*Visual:* AED icon + 'CALL · PUSH · SHOCK'. Calm heartbeat, ECG becomes a flat-then-normal line. Sources card.
